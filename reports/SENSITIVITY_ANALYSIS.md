# Sensitivity and uncertainty analysis

Generated by `project_e sensitivity` — seed 20261022, 2000 Monte Carlo samples.
Dispatch timestep 2 s (baseline verified at 1 s and 0.5 s).

## Method

- **One-at-a-time:** each parameter's declared range is applied at both bounds and
  the full model is rerun. This measures influence, not probability.
- **Monte Carlo:** cost drivers only, uniform over declared ranges, 2000 draws.
  Dispatch and efficiencies stay at baseline, so this is **not** a performance forecast.
- Uniform ranges are uncertainty bounds. Treating them as distributions is an
  assumption made for stress-testing, not evidence.

## What the numbers say

1. **Most influential unknown: `assumptions.missions_per_year`** — moving it across its declared range changes LCOE by up to $2.683/kWh and mass by 0.0 kg. This is the quantity to measure first.
2. At baseline the lowest LCOE is **B3** (TPV + high-temperature thermal store) at $0.288/kWh, but the ranking is not the whole decision: mass, volume and emissions constraints eliminate candidates independently.
3. Over the cost Monte Carlo, **B3** is cheapest most often (82% of draws), with P50 $0.447/kWh. The spread P10–P90 ($0.227–$0.682) is wider than the difference between the top two architectures, so cost assumptions decide the winner.
4. No architecture meets all five targets at baseline. The portable-hybrid branch is therefore an integration and cost problem, not a new-physics problem.

## Limitations

- Cost distributions are assumed uniform and independent; real prices correlate.
- Physics parameters are held at baseline during the Monte Carlo.
- Only one mission is evaluated; conclusions do not transfer to other durations or loads.
- No measured hardware data exists. These are model results (E2/E3).
